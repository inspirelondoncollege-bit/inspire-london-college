Choosing a college in London

What actually matters when you're comparing institutions — beyond the prospectus photos.

Start reading Visit Inspire London College

Picking where to study is a decision that shapes the next several years, yet most students compare colleges the same way they compare phones — by scrolling a homepage and reading a few reviews. A more useful approach is to check the things that actually predict a good experience: accreditation, class sizes, course structure, and what happens to students after they finish.

What to check before you apply

Four questions worth asking directly, before you rely on marketing copy.

Accreditation

Confirm the college and specific course are recognised by the relevant regulatory or awarding body, not just that the college "looks official."

Class sizes and contact hours

Smaller colleges often advertise individual attention — ask how many teaching hours per week that actually translates to.

Progression outcomes

Ask what proportion of students progress to university, employment, or further qualifications, and request the source of that figure.

Support for transfer students

If you're arriving from another system, ask specifically how the college supports that transition.

A smaller-college example

Inspire London College is one example of a smaller, focused institution in London offering accredited courses for students at different stages of their academic path. Colleges at this scale can offer more individual attention than a large university, though — as with any institution — it's worth verifying accreditation and outcomes directly rather than taking marketing copy at face value.

Before you commit: request the college's most recent outcomes data in writing, and check its accreditation status on the awarding body's own website rather than the college's.

"The right college is rarely the one with the best brochure — it's the one whose outcomes you can actually verify." A note on the research behind this guide

Making the decision

There's no universal "best" college — the right one depends on the course, your learning style, and what you want to do afterward. Treat the checklist above as a starting point for questions to ask directly, not a substitute for speaking with current or former students if you can.
